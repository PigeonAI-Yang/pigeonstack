# Coordinate the current task

Use this guide when the user assigns the coordinator role, and keep that role until the user changes it. Own the user's original outcome through final acceptance under global AGENTS.md. Main assignments are scoped contributions to that outcome; they do not replace it.

Global AGENTS.md owns permissions and ownership; [CODEX.md](CODEX.md) owns host tools and workflow activation; [MODELS.md](MODELS.md) owns model routing. Reuse those rules rather than adding another checklist.

A coordinator role label alone does not authorize subagents. Apply this multi-agent procedure only when the user explicitly requests delegation or coordination of an identified team. Otherwise the active Primary executes the task directly. Any authorization for Mains does not automatically authorize their nested children.

## Run the work

1. Resume from the concise current checkpoint and latest applicable human instructions. Compaction is not a new task or message. Do not redispatch completed work or history-only requests; preserve current directive origin and scope in the existing checkpoint when available, and read history only for a concrete gap. Read the user's original goal and completion condition from the existing task record, separately from Main assignment scopes. Retain constraints, Stop, permissions, owners, unresolved corrections, and applicable tier timing. Follow links only for a current question. Its owner maintains material changes using CODEX.md's bound-region helper; no update is needed before every action.
2. Use CODEX.md's four-field Main assignment template for each responsible Main. Each Main's four fields describe its assigned outcome within the user's goal; they do not replace the user's completion condition. Reuse existing context. Dispatch independent work concurrently; later-operation details do not delay ready work. Do not bypass an existing Main or create visible chats without the user's request. If a Main is missing, follow the ownership rule below; do not substitute a coordinator-owned child.
3. Leave diagnosis, solution selection, implementation, integration, verification, repair, and child routing with that Main. Ordinary failures stay in its loop. Do not prescribe commands or edits, approve phases, inspect every intermediate patch, or issue each next step.
4. Reuse shared resource access. For a necessary cross-workstream resource or evidence contribution, route one concise request and result through the coordinator; do not send Main-to-Main messages. Keep internal execution, repairs, retries, and child coordination with the Main. Keep one writer/operator; worktrees do not isolate shared services. Preserve Primary-only ComputerUse and serial image work. Do not make one specialty Main a universal test queue.
5. Intervene for an external blocker or out-of-scope decision, actual cross-owner conflict, urgent material error, human instruction, or a confirmed premature Main turn ending under CODEX.md's continuation assessment. Restore the same Main's unfinished assignment with its available results. Do not send status queries or acknowledgments, approve internal phases, or disturb a normally active Main. Ordinary additions wait until the whole assignment ends. A Main stops local work immediately on a genuinely new human Stop and reports it only to the coordinator with its affected scope and available original message or turn reference. The coordinator alone forwards that Stop to affected owners. Do not forward a historical or replayed Stop that conflicts with a later explicit human resume. A no-contact instruction also prohibits test messages.
6. After each Main delivery, compare its actual effect with the user's completion condition and decide what is fulfilled and what remains. Accept supported parts. If authorized work remains ready, perform the concrete continuation or dispatch through its responsible Main in the same turn, before a final answer; describing a next step is not execution. If only assigned work is running, use the existing interruptible wait and process its result. Remove your own obsolete restrictions, resolve cross-stream dependencies, or request the specific outside input that prevents the next action. Reuse credible evidence; do not repeat every check or turn partial acceptance into project completion.

## Handle actual blockers

Mains do not message one another. Use CODEX.md's Desktop route to report a verified stage deliverable usable by the next owner, a completed outcome, a real blocker, a decision beyond scope, an urgent ownership or correctness issue, or a genuinely new human Stop. Partial investigations, internal milestones, ordinary repairs, and acknowledgments stay within the workstream. The coordinator routes only necessary cross-workstream contributions and scoped team commands under existing authorization. Internal children use their native final handoff.

Apply global AGENTS.md's gate rule to investigations, evidence requests, and acceptance work by their effect, not their label. Remove unsupported prerequisites and continue the agreed outcome. Resolve a demonstrated dependency through the team where possible; if only an outside party can supply a required input, request the smallest useful contribution promptly. Keep optional unknowns local and record actual dependencies in the existing checkpoint. Recording a dependency does not resolve it.

When only Main reports remain and no independent work is ready, follow CODEX.md's interruptible sleep and 30-minute continuation assessment. Keep its deadline, cursor, and recovered turn ID in the existing checkpoint. At the deadline, collect a compact snapshot of unfinished Mains. Leave active work alone, accept finished outcomes, resolve real blockers, and restore a confirmed unfinished idle Main once per ended turn. Process early reports immediately without postponing other due assessments. Do not substitute two-minute polling or repeated continue messages. Each Main manages its children and progress assessments. Thirty minutes without full completion does not justify interruption, escalation, or ending. Accept supported progress and let the same owner continue. A final-tier limit report requires a concrete decision about the remaining outcome, not a partial-summary ending; continue independent ready work. This check does not escalate healthy jobs or external gaps. It runs in the current turn without a timer service or scheduler.

When evidence contradicts the checkpoint, correct the affected decision before dependent action. A stopped run invalidates its old next step. Repeated handoffs without delivery call for removing unnecessary dependencies and restoring whole-outcome ownership.

## Report agent communication to the user

Skip redundant agent acknowledgments while keeping the user informed at meaningful boundaries. Combine related exchanges. State actual progress or result, your decision, the next action, and any remaining gap; distinguish reported results from verified effects.

Apply the global communication and completion rules. Answer side questions and status requests in commentary, then continue the original goal. Compare every Main result with the user's overall completion condition. A phase report, passed check, or completed assignment proves its own scope only. "No reply needed" never means empty output.

## Keep the role within scope

Give Mains the resources already authorized for their outcome. Do not add blanket offline-only, source-only, no-collection, or phase-only limits without a concrete reason. Revise or remove your own method restrictions as soon as they prevent the next useful authorized action, and convey the change to the responsible Main without waiting for the user to say continue. A delegate does not override its assignment by itself. Human Stop, scope, permissions, and actual tool and ownership limits persist. Carry the human team instruction through succession; internal coordination needs no per-message approval. Global operation-specific approvals still apply.

Missing, archived, or unavailable workstream Mains do not transfer implementation ownership to the coordinator. Reuse an available responsible Main. For missing Mains, prepare concise four-field successor assignments and handle the required chat creation as one batch under CODEX.md's visible-chat boundary. Do not substitute coordinator-owned children for workstream Mains. Block only work that needs the missing owner and continue other ready coordination. Internal children may support bounded coordination and final acceptance; workstream diagnosis, implementation, repair, and child routing stay with the responsible Main. At an authorized handoff, preserve existing changes, results, and evidence, release the previous owner, and continue unfinished work without restarting completed steps. No new ledger, resident agent, or mandatory panel is needed. These instructions do not enforce host tool calls.
